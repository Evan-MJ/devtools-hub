What To Include In A Bug Report

A good bug report helps us fix problems fast:

  • Which tool you were using (name or URL).
  • What you expected to happen.
  • What actually happened (and any error message shown).
  • Browser and device (Chrome, Firefox, Safari; desktop or mobile).
  • A screenshot or screen recording when the issue is visual.

Response Times

  • Critical bugs that break tool functionality: fixed as soon as they are found, usually within a few days. We do not publish a fixed SLA — a promise we cannot keep is worse than no promise.
  • Minor issues and feature requests: reviewed weekly.
  • Tool requests: validated against real search demand; popular ideas are shipped as new tools.

Why AI Chatbots Can't Replace Direct Contact

AI chatbots cannot diagnose a broken layout, test a printer, or inspect a pixel-perfect render. When a tool misbehaves on your specific browser, only a human with your exact steps, screenshot, and device details can reproduce and fix it. That is why direct contact exists and why every report is triaged by a person. For general developer questions, authoritative references like the MDN Web Docs and W3C standards are great places to start.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an account to contact you?

No. Contacting us requires no account and no signup. The optional email field is only used if you want a follow-up reply.

Can I request a new developer tool?

Yes. Tell us the tool and how you would use it. We validate requests against real search demand and ship popular ideas as new tools.

Is my contact data shared or sold?

No. We never sell or share personal data. Feedback text is stored only to track and fix issues, and the optional email is used solely to reply. See the Privacy Policy.

What happens after I report a bug?

Your report enters the issue queue and gets triaged by a person. Resolved issues are marked with a green badge on the feedback page.
