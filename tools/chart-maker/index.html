What is a Chart Maker?

A chart maker (also called a graph maker or graph generator) is an online tool that helps you visualize data as bar charts, line graphs, pie charts, and more. Instead of wrestling with spreadsheet software or asking an AI to describe a chart, you type in your labels and values, pick a style, and get a ready-to-export chart that you can drop into presentations, reports, dashboards, or social media posts.

How It Works

Chart.js, a popular open-source library (MIT license), renders the chart directly in your browser using the HTML5 Canvas API. Your data never leaves your device — there is no server, no upload, and no tracking. Every label, color, and value stays local.

Why It Beats AI

Large language models cannot draw. They can describe what a chart might look like, but they cannot produce a pixel-accurate, styled, and exportable chart with your actual data. This tool solves that problem: you get a real chart, not a text description of one.

What You Can Create

Bar charts for comparing categories (sales by quarter), line charts for trends (website traffic over time), pie and doughnut charts for proportions (budget allocation), and radar charts for multi-dimensional comparisons (skill assessments, product scores).

Privacy & Security

Everything runs client-side. Chart.js (342KB minified) loads from a CDN, then all computation happens in your browser tab. Your data is never sent anywhere. Close the tab and nothing remains. No signup, no cookies, no analytics of your chart.

Choosing a chart type that does not mislead

The engine draws exactly the numbers you enter, which means the chart can be perfectly accurate and still communicate the wrong thing. That part is your call.

  • Bar for comparing categories, line for change over time, pie and doughnut for parts of a whole with six slices or fewer, radar for a handful of scored dimensions.
  • A pie chart of twelve slices communicates nothing; the same data as a sorted bar chart reads in one line.
  • Start the value axis at zero for bar charts. A truncated axis exaggerates differences that are not there.
  • Turn on data labels when the exact numbers matter, and drop the legend when there is only one or two series and can be labelled directly.
  • Radar charts compare shapes, not magnitudes. The area of a radar polygon grows with the square of the values, so small differences look much bigger than they are.
  • Write the title as the takeaway, not as the subject: "Bounce rose after the redesign" beats "Bounce rate by month".

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the chart maker really free?

Yes, 100% free. No signup, no credit card, no daily limit, no watermark on exports. You can create unlimited charts. The rendering engine (Chart.js) is open source (MIT) and the page has no ads or upsells.

What chart types are supported?

Bar, Line, Pie, Doughnut, and Radar charts. Bar and Line charts also work with the "Polar Area" option for circular data visualization. More chart types can be added on request — use the feedback link to suggest them.

Can I export my chart as an image?

Yes. Click "Export PNG" to download a high-resolution PNG image of your chart. The export captures the current chart including all colors, labels, title, and legend. The background is transparent for easy placement in presentations or documents.

Do you store my chart data?

No. Your labels, values, and colors stay in your browser's memory for the duration of the session. Closing the tab or refreshing the page clears everything. There is no backend database, no cookies, and no analytics tracking the data you enter.

Can I edit the chart after creating it?

Yes. The chart updates in real time as you add, remove, or change data points, switch chart types, or modify colors. The live preview reflects every change instantly. There is no "save and edit later" — you can export whenever you are satisfied with the preview.

How many data points can I add?

There is no hard limit, but for readability we recommend 3 to 20 data points. Pie and doughnut charts become hard to read beyond 10-12 slices. Bar and line charts can handle 20+ data points comfortably. The canvas auto-scales to fit your data.

Can I use the exported charts commercially?

Yes. The charts you create are yours. Chart.js is MIT licensed, so the rendering engine imposes no restrictions. You can use exported charts in client presentations, reports, social media, websites, or any commercial context without attribution.
