Free Click Speed Test with CPS and Reaction Time

This free click speed test measures how many times you can click in a set time and reports the result as clicks per second, or CPS. You can test your left click, right click, spacebar presses, or touch taps over runs from 1 second up to 100 seconds. The page also includes a reaction time test that times how quickly you can respond to a green screen. Everything is measured locally in your browser, nothing is uploaded, and there is no signup.

Why AI Chatbots Cannot Measure Your Click Speed

An AI chatbot can explain what a CPS test is and repeat general advice about clicking techniques, but it cannot take a real measurement. It has no access to your mouse, keyboard, or the timing of your actual presses. When you ask a chatbot "how fast can I click?", you get text, not a number. This tool solves that problem by simply counting the real events your browser receives and dividing them by the exact elapsed time. The number you see is a fact about your own performance, measured by your own device.

How to Use

Pick an input mode and a duration, then click the large area to start. The timer begins on your first click and that click counts toward your total. Keep clicking as fast as you can until time runs out. While you click you can watch the live CPS, the clicks count, and a per-second breakdown of your pace. When the run ends, the result shows your total clicks, your CPS, clicks per minute, your best one-second burst, and an approximate rating. Your personal best for each duration is kept in your browser.

For the spacebar mode, press the spacebar to start and keep pressing it to count. For right-click mode, right-click the area (the browser menu is suppressed during the test). The touch mode works with taps on phones and tablets. The reaction time test works differently: click once to arm it, wait for the box to turn green, then click as fast as possible. It runs five trials and averages them.

What Is a Good CPS?

Published click-speed research from popular test sites reports that a normal clicker averages around 6 to 8 CPS in a 10-second run. Averaging 10 CPS or more is considered fast, and skilled players using advanced techniques can push well beyond that. The techniques are generally described like this: regular clicking around 3 to 9 CPS, jitter clicking around 10 to 14 CPS by tensing the forearm, butterfly clicking around 15 to 25 CPS by alternating two fingers, and drag clicking up to 25-100 CPS with friction-based drags. The one-second result is always higher than the ten-second result, because holding a fast pace over time is harder than a short burst.

Common Uses

Gamers use the tool to measure and train their clicking speed for Minecraft PvP, first-person shooters, and other competitive titles. People test a new mouse to see if it registers fast clicks reliably. The spacebar mode is popular as a quick skill test, and the reaction mode is used by drivers, pilots, and esports players to practice quick responses. Students often use it as a fun classroom challenge between friends. There is no account, so results are private by design.

How Accuracy and Automation Are Handled

The tool counts clicks using the browser events your device actually produces and times the window with the high-resolution clock, so the CPS is exact for the run. A best-one-second value is computed as the largest number of clicks that fits inside any single one-second window. The tool also flags a clear sign of automation: if many consecutive clicks arrive at intervals that are almost perfectly identical, it displays a note that the pattern looks automated. An auto-clicker will still produce a number, but the note makes the result easy to trust less.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does CPS mean?

CPS stands for clicks per second. It is your total number of clicks divided by the test duration. CPM is the same number expressed per minute.

Why is my 10-second CPS lower than my 1-second CPS?

Clicking speed naturally drops as the test gets longer. In a short burst you can go as fast as your fingers physically allow, but muscle fatigue and lost rhythm lower the average over a longer window. Most people see a noticeably lower score at 10 seconds or 60 seconds than at 1 second.

Is a higher CPS cheating in games?

That depends on the game. Some Minecraft PvP servers allow high CPS achieved by hand (jitter, butterfly, or drag clicking) but ban auto-clickers, which is why this tool flags an automation pattern so your hand-measured result is easy to verify. Check the rules of the server or game you play.

How can I increase my click speed?

Practice regularly, keep your wrist relaxed, and use a mouse that fits your hand. Once regular clicking is smooth, players often learn jitter clicking (vibrating the forearm), butterfly clicking (two fingers alternating), or drag clicking (finger friction on the button). Each technique takes time to master and can strain your hand, so take breaks.

Does the tool work on mobile?

Yes. Choose the touch mode and tap the area as fast as you can. Reaction time also works with taps on a phone or tablet.

Are my results stored or shared?

No. All counting happens in your browser. Your personal bests are saved only in your own browser's local storage. Nothing is sent to a server, and there is no account or tracking.

What is a good reaction time?

Most people average between 250 and 350 ms. Under 250 ms is quick, and results under 200 ms are exceptional. A very slow average over 400 ms is normal for out-of-practice or tired reflexes and improves with training.

What is the Kohi test?

The Kohi click test is simply a 10-second CPS test. It got its name from the Kohi Minecraft server, where players compared how fast they could click over a ten-second window during PvP. This page's 10-second preset is that same test.
