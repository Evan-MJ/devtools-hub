Race Time Predictor

The Riegel formula (T₂ = T₁ × (D₂/D₁)^1.06) uses your recent race result to predict finish times for other distances. For example, a 25-minute 5K predicts a 51:57 10K, a 1:54:39 half marathon, and a 3:59:13 marathon. The 1.06 exponent accounts for the fatigue factor — as distance increases, your pace naturally slows.

Training Pace Zones

Your race time determines six training zones: Recovery, Easy, Tempo, Threshold, Intervals, and Repetitions. Each zone targets a specific physiological adaptation. Training at the right pace prevents over-training and maximizes performance gains.

How do I calculate my running pace?

Divide your total time (in minutes) by your total distance. For example, if you run 10 km in 50 minutes, your pace is 5:00 per kilometer (50 ÷ 10 = 5.0 minutes = 5:00). To convert to minutes per mile, multiply your km pace by 1.609.

What is the Riegel formula for race prediction?

The Riegel formula predicts finish times: T₂ = T₁ × (D₂/D₁)^1.06. T₁ is your known time for distance D₁. The exponent 1.06 accounts for the fatigue factor — longer distances require slightly slower pace. This formula is most accurate for distances between 1,500m and the marathon.

What training paces should I run?

Based on your current race fitness: Recovery (>5:00/km slower than 5K pace), Easy (3:30-5:00/km slower), Tempo (15-20 sec/km slower than 5K pace), Threshold (10K pace), Interval (3K-5K pace), Repetition (mile to 3K pace). These zones build different energy systems.

What is a good running pace for beginners?

A comfortable conversational pace for most beginners is 6:00-7:30 per km (9:40-12:00 per mile). For a 5K race, beginners typically finish in 30-40 minutes (6:00-8:00/km). Use the "Find Time" mode above to see what pace matches your goal.

How do I convert min/km to min/mile?

Multiply your minutes-per-kilometer pace by 1.609 to get minutes-per-mile. For example, 5:00/km × 1.609 = 8:03/mile. Use the Converter tab above for instant conversions between all pace and speed units.

What is a negative split in running?

A negative split means running the second half of a race faster than the first half. Most world records are set using negative splits because it prevents early fatigue. Use the Splits tab to plan your pacing strategy with even or negative splits.

How accurate is the Riegel race predictor?

The Riegel formula is accurate within 1-3% for distances close to your reference race (e.g., 10K predicting half marathon). Accuracy drops to 5-10% for very different distances (e.g., 5K predicting marathon). For best results, use a race distance close to your target.
