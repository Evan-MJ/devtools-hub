Why cm/360°, Not eDPI?

eDPI (DPI × sensitivity) only works within the same game. CS2 uses 0.022°/count, Valorant uses 0.07°/count. The same eDPI means completely different physical turn distances.

cm/360° matches physical mouse movement — your muscle memory transfers perfectly. 280 Valorant eDPI ≠ 280 CS2 eDPI. Always convert using cm/360°.

How the Converter Works

The converter uses the 360° distance method (cm/360°). It calculates how far your mouse physically moves for a full 360° turn in your source game, then finds the sensitivity in your target game that produces the exact same physical distance. Your muscle memory stays consistent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my aim feel different even after perfect conversion?

FOV, aspect ratio, monitor size, game animations, and movement speed all affect perceived sensitivity. The 360° distance matches perfectly; the difference you feel is visual, not mechanical.

Should I change my DPI when switching games?

No. Keep your hardware DPI constant. Only change DPI in the converter if you genuinely use different DPIs in different games. The converter accounts for it automatically.

What is cm/360°?

Centimeters per 360° turn. The physical distance your mouse travels on the pad for one complete in-game rotation. It's the only measurement that transfers across games.

Does this work for ADS sensitivity?

This converter matches hipfire sensitivity. ADS (aim down sights) multipliers vary by game and optic. Match hipfire first, then adjust ADS per game feel.

Is my data saved anywhere?

Only your Favorites are saved in your browser's localStorage. No data is sent to any server. Clear favorites anytime.

How accurate are the yaw values?

Yaw constants are sourced from game engine documentation, community reverse-engineering, and developer confirmation. Verified as of 2026. Always 360°-test in practice range.
