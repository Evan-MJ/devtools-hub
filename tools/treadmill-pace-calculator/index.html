How This Treadmill Pace Calculator Works

Treadmills display speed in MPH or km/h, but runners think in pace — minutes per mile or minutes per kilometer. This calculator bridges that gap with exact conversions and practical training data.

Core Conversion

  • MPH to min/mile: 60 ÷ MPH = minutes per mile (e.g., 60 ÷ 6.0 = 10:00/mi)
  • km/h to min/km: 60 ÷ km/h = minutes per kilometer (e.g., 60 ÷ 9.66 = 6:12/km)
  • Cross-unit: 1 mph = 1.60934 km/h, 1 mile = 1.60934 km

5K & 10K Finish Times

The chart and converter show even-split finish times if you hold that belt speed continuously. Important: These are treadmill workout durations at constant speed, not outdoor race predictions. Outdoor running adds wind, turns, and grade variation that a treadmill does not model.

Effort Levels

  • Walk (2.0–4.0 mph / 3.2–6.4 km/h): Recovery, warm-up, cool-down. Full conversation possible.
  • Jog (4.5–5.5 mph / 7.2–8.8 km/h): Easy running for beginners; very easy day for experienced runners.
  • Run (6.0+ mph / 9.6+ km/h): Continuous running for people who already run. 7.5 mph / 12 km/h ≈ tempo.

Grade-Adjusted Pace (Incline)

When you set an incline, the converter shows your "flat equivalent" pace — the pace you'd run on a flat surface at the same effort. Based on the common approximation: each 1% incline adds ~12–15 seconds per mile to your pace at the same physiological effort.

Calorie Estimate

Uses ACSM metabolic equations for running/walking on a treadmill. Accounts for speed, grade, weight, and duration. Results are estimates — actual calorie burn varies by individual metabolism, running economy, and body composition.

How do I convert treadmill MPH to minutes per mile?

Divide 60 by the MPH. For example: 60 ÷ 6.0 mph = 10:00 per mile. 60 ÷ 7.5 mph = 8:00 per mile. Use the Converter tab above for instant results.

What's the difference between treadmill speed and outdoor pace?

Treadmill speed is belt speed with no wind resistance and perfect footing. Outdoor pace includes wind, turns, elevation changes, and surface variation. A 6:00/mile on a treadmill typically feels easier than a 6:00/mile outdoors. Do not treat treadmill 5K/10K columns as outdoor race predictions.

How accurate is the calorie estimate?

The ACSM equation is a scientific standard but individual variation is 10–20%. Factors not modeled: running economy, body composition, fitness level, temperature, and whether you hold the handrails (which reduces burn significantly). Treat as a relative guide, not an absolute number.

What incline should I use to simulate outdoor running?

A common guideline is 1% incline to offset the lack of wind resistance at speeds above ~7 mph. Below that, 0% is fine. For grade-adjusted effort, use the Incline field in the Converter — it shows your flat-equivalent pace at the same effort.

Can I print the pace chart for the gym?

Yes. Click the "Print Chart" button in the Full Chart tab. The print layout hides navigation and controls, giving you a clean reference card for the treadmill rail.

What is a "10-minute mile" on a treadmill?

6.0 mph. It's the most common easy-run setting on US treadmills. At 6.0 mph, a 5K takes 31:04 and a 10K takes 1:02:08 if you hold that speed continuously.
