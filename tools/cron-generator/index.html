How Cron Expressions Work

Cron expressions are concise scheduling notation used across Linux, Unix, and many web services. A cron expression consists of 5 fields separated by spaces, representing minute, hour, day of month, month, and day of week.

Cron Expression Format

* * * * *
  • 1st field (Minute): 0-59
  • 2nd field (Hour): 0-23
  • 3rd field (Day): 1-31
  • 4th field (Month): 1-12
  • 5th field (Weekday): 0-6 (Sun-Sat)

Special Characters

CharacterMeaningExample
*Any value* * * * * = every minute
,Value list0,30 * * * * = at 0 and 30 minutes
-Range9-17 * * * * = every hour 9-5
/Step*/15 * * * * = every 15 minutes

Common Cron Patterns

0 * * * * - Every hour at minute 0
0 0 * * * - Every day at midnight
0 9 * * 1-5 - Weekdays at 9 AM
*/15 * * * * - Every 15 minutes
0 0 1 * * - First day of every month
0 0 * * 0 - Every Sunday at midnight

Cron in Different Systems

Linux/Unix Cron: System services, backups, log rotation. Edit with crontab -e.

Cloudflare Workers: Scheduled tasks using cron_trigger in wrangler.toml.

GitHub Actions: on: schedule: cron: '0 * * * *'

AWS CloudWatch: EventBridge rules with cron expressions for Lambda triggers.

Jenkins: Build triggers with cron syntax for CI/CD pipelines.

Timezone Considerations

Cron expressions run in the server's timezone, which often defaults to UTC. A cron job scheduled for 0 9 * * * runs at 9 AM UTC, not 9 AM local time unless the system is configured otherwise. This causes unexpected execution times when servers span multiple timezones.
