How the Lease Payment Is Calculated

A lease is not a loan on the whole car. You finance the part of the car that is expected to disappear during the term, plus a finance charge on the money the lender has tied up. The calculator follows the standard money-factor method that dealers and lenders use, and it shows each step so you can check a quote line by line.

  1. Residual value = MSRP × residual percentage.
  2. Capitalized cost = negotiated price + acquisition, doc and other fees − down payment − rebate − trade-in equity. Negative equity is added instead of subtracted.
  3. Depreciation = (capitalized cost − residual value) ÷ lease term in months.
  4. Rent charge = (capitalized cost + residual value) × money factor.
  5. Base payment = depreciation + rent charge.
  6. Monthly tax = base payment × tax rate, when your state taxes each payment.
  7. Due at signing = down payment + first month's payment + any tax collected upfront.
  8. Total lease cost = down payment + monthly payment × term + upfront tax + disposition fee.

With the defaults on this page, a $40,000 car at a $38,000 negotiated price, a 55% residual, a 0.00125 money factor, 36 months and $3,000 down gives a residual of $22,000, a capitalized cost of $36,094, depreciation of $391.50, a rent charge of $72.62 and a base payment of $464.12. At 7% monthly tax the payment is about $496.61, the amount due at signing is about $3,496.61, and the total lease cost including a $395 disposition fee is about $21,272.96. Small changes in the money factor or the negotiated price move the payment more than most buyers expect.

Lease vs Buy: What the Comparison Measures

The comparison uses cash flow, not sticker price. For the lease it adds the down payment and every monthly payment, then subtracts nothing because you return the car. For the purchase it adds the down payment and every loan payment, then subtracts the resale value you still own at the end. The difference is the net cost of each path over the same number of months.

The break-even resale value is the number that makes the two paths equal. If you think the car will be worth more than that figure at the end of the term, buying and selling wins. If it will be worth less, or if you would rather not own a depreciating asset, leasing can cost less. The calculator prints that break-even figure in the result note so you can test your own resale assumption instead of accepting a default.

What the Payment Does Not Include

Insurance, maintenance, registration, excess-mileage charges, wear-and-tear fees and early-termination penalties are outside the payment. A refundable security deposit is also excluded because you get it back. The disposition fee is included in the total lease cost but not in the monthly payment, and it is often waived when you lease another car from the same lender. Add a broker fee or a document fee your dealer charges to the other-fees field, or the total will be low.

Why This Beats Asking an AI Chatbot

A language model usually treats the money factor as an interest rate, forgets that the rent charge is calculated on the capitalized cost plus the residual, and applies sales tax to the whole car instead of the monthly payment. It also cannot hold your actual quote while you change one number at a time. This page runs the same arithmetic on every visit, shows the depreciation and rent charge separately, converts the money factor to an APR, and lets you replace every assumption with the figure on your dealer's worksheet. The result is a number you can check, not a paragraph you have to trust.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a money factor on a car lease?

The money factor is the lease finance rate written as a decimal that is easy to multiply. Multiply it by 2,400 to get the equivalent APR. A money factor of 0.00125 is 3.00% APR. Lenders quote it this way because the rent charge is calculated on the average of the capitalized cost and the residual value.

How do I convert a money factor to an interest rate?

Multiply the money factor by 2,400. For example, 0.00050 becomes 1.20%, 0.00125 becomes 3.00%, and 0.00250 becomes 6.00%. The result is the finance-rate equivalent, not the full APR, because a lease APR can also include fees.

What is a residual value on a lease?

The residual value is the price the lender expects the car to be worth at the end of the lease, usually written as a percentage of the MSRP. A 55% residual on a $40,000 car is $22,000. A higher residual lowers the depreciation part of the payment, so the same car can lease for less when the lender sets a stronger residual.

Is it cheaper to lease or buy a car?

It depends on how long you keep the car and what it is worth at the end. This calculator compares the cash you put in, down payment plus monthly payments, minus the resale value you keep when you buy. Leasing usually wins if you return the car before the loan is paid off or if the resale value falls below the break-even figure shown. Buying usually wins if you keep the car longer than the lease term.

What is due at signing on a car lease?

Due at signing normally includes the first month's payment, any capitalized cost reduction, and taxes or fees your state collects upfront. This calculator adds those to the down payment and first payment you enter. It does not include a refundable security deposit, registration, or a broker fee unless you add them to the other-fees field.

How is sales tax calculated on a lease?

Most states tax the monthly payment, so the tax is the base payment times the local rate. Some states collect tax on the total of the payments at signing, and a few tax the full selling price. Choose the method that matches your state. The calculator shows the monthly tax or the upfront tax, not both.

What happens if I have negative equity in my trade-in?

Negative equity is the amount you still owe on the trade-in above what it is worth. The calculator adds that difference to the capitalized cost, which raises the monthly payment. Positive equity works the other way and lowers the capitalized cost.

Why is a lease payment not just the depreciation?

Because the lender also charges a rent charge for the money tied up in the car. The rent charge is the capitalized cost plus the residual value, multiplied by the money factor. On a 36-month lease that charge can add $50 to $150 a month, which is why comparing only depreciation understates the payment.

Does the calculator include insurance, maintenance or mileage overage?

No. Insurance, maintenance, registration, excess-mileage charges and wear-and-tear fees are separate. The payment shown is the depreciation, rent charge and tax on the lease you described.
