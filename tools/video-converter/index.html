How it works

Drop a video file on the page, choose the format you need, and press Convert. The tool reads the file with the File API, hands it to a WebAssembly build of FFmpeg that runs in the tab, re-encodes it for the destination format, and downloads the result. Because the engine runs client side, the file never touches a server.

Why format conversion matters

The same clip can be blocked by the player you already use. A screen recording saved as MOV may not open in an HTML5 video tag, a phone export saved as MKV may not play in an app that expects MP4, and a screen recording saved as MP4 may be far larger than the platform allows. Converting to the container and codec a target actually accepts removes that friction without re-encoding by hand.

Private by design

Nothing about your file travels anywhere. The video is read into the tab and encoded in WebAssembly on your device, then written back to a download you trigger yourself. Once the page and the engine are loaded, no data moves on the network.

Honest limits

Re-encoding is real work and it happens on your machine, so it scales with the file. A long clip takes minutes rather than seconds because the encoder is CPU bound in WebAssembly. Output is re-encoded rather than stream copied, so the source codec may not be preserved. Files much over about 500 MB may exceed what the tab can hold in memory during a re-encode; for anything bigger a desktop encoder is the right tool.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert a video from MP4 to WebM?

Drop the MP4 into the converter, pick WebM as the target format, and press Convert. The tool re-encodes the video and audio with the codec that WebM uses and downloads the result.

Is my video uploaded to a server when I convert it?

No. The file is read into the tab and handed to a WebAssembly build of FFmpeg running in the browser. Once the engine loads, no data moves to a server.

Which formats can I convert between?

The tool converts between common modern containers such as MP4, WebM, MOV, MKV and AVI, choosing the matching codec for each target.

How long does a conversion take?

Conversion decodes and re-encodes the video on your own device, so it scales with the file. Short clips finish in seconds; longer clips take minutes because the encoder is CPU bound in WebAssembly.

Can I convert a video that is too large?

The in-browser engine handles files up to about 500 MB. Larger files may exceed what the tab can hold in memory during a re-encode, and for anything bigger a desktop encoder is the honest answer.
