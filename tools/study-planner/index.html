How the Study Planner Works

The planner takes your subject list with priority levels, the exam date, and your available hours per day. It then runs a deterministic scheduling algorithm that distributes study blocks across 7 days using three principles from cognitive science: (1) spacing - same subject never appears on consecutive days (spaced repetition), (2) interleaving - hard subjects are alternated with easy subjects in each day, (3) time-of-day optimization - hard subjects get morning slots, easy subjects get evening slots, based on research that cognitive performance peaks in late morning. The schedule can be exported as PNG image, printed directly, copied as plain text, or downloaded as an .ics file for calendar apps.

Why AI Chatbots Cannot Replace This Tool

An AI chatbot can write a paragraph of study advice, but it cannot generate a 7-day grid with specific time slots, enforce cognitive science scheduling principles, calculate session counts based on priority and days remaining, or produce a downloadable .ics file. Asking ChatGPT for a study plan produces variable output every time - the same prompt yields different schedules. The Study Planner uses a deterministic algorithm so the same inputs always produce the same schedule. This is exactly what students preparing for exams need: a stable, reproducible plan they can commit to, adjust, and follow day by day.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the priority system work?

High-priority subjects get 4 sessions per week plus a final review session in the last 3 days before the exam. Medium-priority subjects get 2-3 sessions per week. Low-priority subjects get 1 review session. Sessions are weighted by difficulty: hard subjects get longer Pomodoro blocks.

Can I edit the schedule after it's generated?

The schedule is deterministic from your inputs. If you want a different layout, change the subject priorities or hours-per-day and click Generate again. The exported plain-text version is fully editable in any text editor or notes app. For full editing in-browser, use the print-to-PDF feature with your browser's PDF editor.

What is the iCal export?

iCal (.ics) is a universal calendar file format. Clicking Export iCal downloads a .ics file that you can import into Google Calendar (Add other calendars > Import), Apple Calendar (File > Import), Outlook (File > Open > Import), or any calendar app that supports the iCalendar standard. Each study session becomes a calendar event with subject name and notes.

Why are hard subjects in the morning?

Research on circadian rhythm shows that working memory and analytical thinking peak between 9-11 AM for most people. Hard subjects like math, physics, or programming require these cognitive functions, so they get the morning slots. Easy subjects like review or reading get evening slots when fatigue is higher.

Does it work for school, university, and self-study?

Yes. The algorithm does not care whether the subjects are math, biology, history, programming, or language. Set your priorities based on difficulty and exam weight, set the exam date, and the scheduler distributes sessions based on the cognitive science principles.

What if I have multiple exams on different dates?

The planner supports one exam date per schedule. For multiple exams, generate a schedule for your nearest exam first. After that exam passes, regenerate with the next exam date. Subjects not on your nearest exam should be marked low priority so they get only review sessions.
