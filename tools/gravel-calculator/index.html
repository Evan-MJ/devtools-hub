How This Gravel Calculator Works

The calculator multiplies the area of the shape by the depth to get a volume in cubic feet. A rectangle or square uses length times width; a circle uses pi times the radius squared. Dividing cubic feet by 27 gives cubic yards, and multiplying cubic yards by the bulk density of the material gives the weight, which is then divided by 2,000 to report US short tons.

Bulk material is sold three different ways and the calculator reports all three. Yards are the volume unit most suppliers quote for delivery, tons are the weight unit most quarries quote, and bags are what a home centre sells for small jobs. Because 0.5 cubic foot and 2 cubic foot bags are both common, both counts are shown. The waste factor is applied before every output, so the bags and the tons already include it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much gravel do I need?

Multiply the area in square feet by the depth in feet to get cubic feet, then divide by 27 to get cubic yards. A 20 by 30 foot driveway at 3 inches deep is 600 square feet times 0.25 feet, or 150 cubic feet, which is about 5.6 cubic yards before the waste factor.

How many tons are in a cubic yard of gravel?

Most loose gravel weighs about 2,700 to 3,000 pounds per cubic yard, which is roughly 1.35 to 1.5 US short tons. Pea gravel is near the low end at about 1.4 tons per yard and crusher run near the high end at about 1.5 tons per yard. Moisture and source rock move the number by ten to fifteen percent, so the calculator lets you enter your supplier's density.

How many square feet does a ton of gravel cover?

At a 2 inch depth, one ton of gravel at 1.4 tons per cubic yard covers about 115 square feet. The same ton covers about 58 square feet at 4 inches and about 38 square feet at 6 inches. Deeper layers always cover less area per ton.

How deep should gravel be for a driveway?

A pedestrian path needs about 2 to 3 inches of gravel over a weed barrier. A residential driveway usually needs a 4 inch compacted base of crusher run with 2 to 4 inches of decorative gravel on top, so plan for 6 to 8 inches of total depth before compaction.

Should I add a waste factor?

Yes. Uneven subgrade, spillage, and the reduction of loose gravel thickness are covered by a waste factor of 5 to 10 percent. A driveway or a large bed can be specified with ten percent as the safe default option, while small paths may opt for five percent.

Why use this calculator instead of asking an AI chatbot?

An AI chatbot cannot weigh a material it has never seen and often guesses bag counts or skips the density step. This calculator runs the same arithmetic every time, shows the density it used, converts cubic yards to tons and to bags, and lets you override the density with the figure your supplier quotes.
